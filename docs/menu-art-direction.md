# Menu art direction

IRONWAKE's menus are a **field-service hangar on the Cinder Front**. The player inhabits a place, owns a machine, and prepares a sortie. The machine and environment carry the image; controls punctuate it.

## Visual language

- Dirty ivory markings on warm black steel, with vermillion reserved for selection, action, and warnings. Cold light belongs primarily to mech optics and equipment. Avoid adding competing accent colors.
- Use real game geometry, authored camera framing, directional light, shadows, and hangar structures. Give the mech a readable silhouette and room around its limbs. A generic atmospheric picture behind a dashboard does not meet this direction.
- Use Arial/Helvetica at normal width throughout. Keep useful section names and actual data labels. Do not add uppercase eyebrows, numbered captions, invented telemetry, or decorative microcopy to fill space.
- Put the home choices in a restrained typographic menu beside the machine. Remove landing-page slogans, feature counters, promotional cards, and application navigation chrome.
- Treat fitting as a closer view of the same place. Socket selection reveals a focused component list and a compact maintenance docket. The machine remains the subject during comparison and installation.
- Treat operations as a mission map and service dossier, with one clear deployment decision. Shared materials and selection behavior connect the views; identical grids need not.
- Selection needs a strong position/shape change, readable focus, and short mechanical motion. Entering a view or installing a part can move the camera or machine deliberately. Respect reduced-motion preferences and do not delay interaction for presentation.

## References actually inspected

These are principles to study, not styles to combine or assets to reuse.

- **Titanfall 2 (2016):** [menu screenshot and display comparisons](https://www.wsgf.org/dr/titanfall-2/en). One compact text menu sits beside a dominant helmet and a coherent amber-lit scene. Quiet unselected rows and a crisp selected state establish hierarchy without a screen of containers.
- **Metro Exodus (2019):** [main-menu environment author's portfolio](https://www.artstation.com/artwork/6akKnW), [implemented console screenshot](https://www.pcgamer.com/our-favorite-title-screens-and-main-menus-in-pc-gaming/). The console's switches, labels, materials, and surrounding cabin belong to one inhabited world. Its author describes the environment changing with story progression. Study that specificity; IRONWAKE does not need to reproduce the literal train console.
- **Destiny (2014):** [character equipment screenshot](https://www.mobygames.com/game/68327/destiny/screenshots/playstation-4/757704/). A full-height character anchors compact gear slots. Open space, equipment imagery, and limited framing give the equipped subject priority.
- **NieR: Automata (2017):** [official UI designer's explanation with screenshots](https://www.platinumgames.co.jp/dev-nier-automata/article/183). The designer describes an initially bland flat foundation, a consistent musical-score motif, monitor texture/lens treatment, and carefully tuned animation. The relevant lesson is an internally coherent visual language, rather than copying its beige palette or ornament.

## Visual review gates

Functional checks are necessary, but cannot establish art quality. Review implemented screenshots and interactions at desktop, phone portrait, and short landscape sizes.

1. **Identity:** hide the text. The composition should still suggest a mech game and a service hangar, rather than a website template.
2. **Focal hierarchy:** squint at the screen. The machine/world is the dominant visual mass, and one current action or selection is obvious. Supporting labels do not compete with it.
3. **Space:** fitting leaves roughly 60% of the desktop composition available to the machine. Neither a boxed preview nor a wall of component cards should overpower it. Smaller screens preserve a useful view of the subject while making the current task reachable.
4. **Restraint:** every panel, border, label, and icon earns its place. Empty space is intentional; avoid invented data and repeated instructions used as filler.
5. **Interaction:** clickable, selected, fitted, previewed, unavailable, and warning states remain distinguishable through form, position, text, or motion as well as color. Keyboard focus is visible.
6. **Continuity:** lighting, typography, materials, registration details, and motion describe the same place across home, assembly, and operations.
7. **Readable scale:** useful labels stay legible at normal viewing size. There is no decorative microtype pretending to be functional information.
8. **Purposeful motion:** transitions explain a change of place or selection; installation visibly changes the machine. Effects settle quickly and do not obstruct input.

Reject screenshots that only add grime, a new palette, large typography, or an atmospheric backdrop to the previous card-and-sidebar composition. The composition, control vocabulary, and relationship to the game world must agree.
