# Ironwake industrial visual pass

This draft implements a focused rendering pass over the current integrated PR stack. It needs fresh rendered acceptance before it can be called visually ready. Research and repository inspection were performed on 26 October 2026.

## Existing game and art direction

Ironwake is a browser mech-combat game with nine short campaign sorties and a recurring walkable 3D home, Wake Anchorage. Its strongest existing language is procedural industrial anime: heavy mechanical silhouettes, ink edges, cel shading, exaggerated anticipation, warm armor and cool instruments. Ashgate's stacked cargo, Glasswater's pipes, the relay masts, foundry stacks, rail machinery, crystals and Crown Engine establish authored environmental identities. This pass extends that language with the existing geometry rather than introducing unrelated photorealistic assets.

The source renderer vendors **Three.js r170**, confirmed through `THREE.REVISION`. Current upstream documentation describes a later release, so implementation-sensitive shader behavior was also checked against r170 source. `three-view.js` already has a moving 1024-square directional shadow map, hemisphere light, fog, pooled hit/explosion effects, and extensive equipment effects. `hub-scene.js` has its own shadowed light rig and physical station presentation. Existing camera effects are applied after the aim ray; enemy intent and essential danger cues already work in Reduced mode. Another layer of flashes, speed lines, camera shake, bloom or fog sheets would compete with those systems.

Local primary references inspected include [campaign content](campaign-missions.md), [enemy animation and telegraphs](enemy-behaviors.md), [equipment/chassis integration](chassis-integration.md), [hub/career flow](career-progression.md), [field technology](field-progression.md), and [module ownership](parallel-development.md), plus the rendering modules themselves.

The earlier local direction pass at `40a07bf4a8364e403f244dab3af7d0402a1d696a` was inspected, including its report, manifest and historical desktop combat image. Its arena-dressing vocabulary is reused and attributed in the manifest. Its older PR merges, gameplay pacing, UI styles and progression fixes are not replayed over the newer stack. The earlier image was a reference, not a capture of this branch.

## Research and decisions

| Primary source | Guidance used | Decision for Ironwake |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Three.js LightShadow](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/LightShadow.html) and [r170 implementation](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r170/src/lights/LightShadow.js) | Shadow-map resolution has a computation cost; bias has artifact tradeoffs; shadow resources require disposal. | Retain one shadow caster and the existing PCF path, add a 512-square compact budget, and snap the moving map in the light's view plane. Soft contact projections supplement coarse shadow maps. No extra shadowed point lights. |
| [Three.js InstancedMesh](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/InstancedMesh.html) | Repeated transforms and colors can share geometry/material draws; instance resources need explicit disposal. | Batch static arena colors by geometry and lit/unlit mode. Use fixed instanced pools for 52 contacts, 24/8 scorch marks and practical light pools. Dispose those pools on rebuild/return. |
| [MDN WebGL best practices](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/WebGL_API/WebGL_best_practices) | Batch draws, budget backing-buffer pixels, avoid blocking queries and release resources eagerly. | Keep the combat DPR ceiling at 1.35, use 1.0 on coarse-pointer or constrained devices, and avoid render targets, synchronous GPU queries and postprocessing. The hub shares the conservative ceiling. |
| [Three.js Fog](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/Fog.html) | Linear fog can preserve a clear near field while blending distant scenery. | Keep the existing combat fog range of 160–650 render units. Atmosphere comes from distant silhouettes and a sparse peripheral particle volume, without increasing near-field haze. |
| [Three.js PointsMaterial](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/PointsMaterial.html) | Points provide a compact particle path with depth attenuation; maps need deliberate color handling. | Use one small depth-tested Points batch, 96/32 motes, with a procedural mask, no depth writes and a clear radius around the pilot. Classic Cinderfall retains its existing ash instead of receiving another volume. |
| [r170 output-color shader](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r170/src/renderers/shaders/ShaderChunk/colorspace_fragment.glsl.js) and [tone-mapping shader](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r170/src/renderers/shaders/ShaderChunk/tonemapping_fragment.glsl.js) | Custom shader output must pass through the renderer's output conversion. | Correct the campaign sky shader to use those chunks. Surface pools modify the standard Basic shader's alpha while retaining its map, fog and output stages. No engine upgrade or global tone-mapping change. |
| [W3C motion guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html) | Users need control over unnecessary animation. | Honor the existing saved Full/Reduced preference and OS-derived default. Reduced completely hides ambient motes and retains only four quiet scorch marks. Contact shadows stay as spatial cues. This is a design choice informed by the guidance, not a WCAG certification. |

The resulting art direction is warm practical light against cool steel, smoky depth beyond the fighting space, and believable contact under heavy machines. Cyan remains allied instrumentation, amber remains interaction/objective language, and existing coral/red danger cues remain dominant. New marks are neutral and soft-edged, so they do not resemble the existing attack rings.

## Implementation

- `visual-quality.js` owns conservative device budgets, environment palette families and light-plane shadow snapping. A coarse pointer, at most four CPU cores, or at most 4 GiB reported memory selects compact settings. Missing hints keep the standard ceiling. These hints are conservative heuristics, not measured device speed.
- `battle-lighting.js` adds an unshadowed cool fill and warm/cool mission-family keys. Space/workshop lighting stays on its existing palette. The directional light remains the only shadow caster; the existing bias and camera coverage are preserved.
- `surface-pool.js` supplies fixed instanced quads with per-instance opacity and 32-square procedural masks. No external art, paid assets, additional framebuffer or light pass is introduced.
- `ground-contact.js` projects living machines onto actual cover, decks and ramp slopes. Shadows soften and widen with flight altitude and shrink at elevated edges. Exact surface footprints avoid the movement system's padded cover margins. Explosion marks are bounded, fade over 14 simulation seconds, reject airbursts/landings/cover/ramp edges and deduplicate repeated replicated events. They reset on time rewind or battlefield teardown. No simulation or network state is written.
- `ambient-air.js` adds sparse low-opacity industrial air outside a 22-unit pilot radius. The hub uses the same vocabulary. Reduced mode hides it and stops buffer updates. Effects cannot intercept aiming rays.
- `arena-dressing.js` adapts the earlier local pass. Boundary piers were adapted into thin cladding on the existing 6.5-unit wall faces; distant infrastructure stays outside the playable area. The old extra objective bases were omitted to preserve warning/objective legibility. Sparse service markings and seams replace the dense test-grid rhythm.
- `round-scene.js` shares static instance colors across geometry/light batches and prevents emissive dressing from casting shadows. Collider registration, authored landmarks, objectives, portals and extraction remain intact. `hub-scene.js` retains station navigation, contextual panel framing and the campaign/deployment flow.

The old gameplay/UI branch is left untouched. The visual-specific diff is against PR19's exact head; the PR against canonical main necessarily contains its outstanding dependencies and the formatting/module migration.
